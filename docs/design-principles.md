# Design Principles

EmergencyUNA is a contingency-planning framework, not an entity-formation package. The core drafting challenge is to give a DAO a credible emergency response mechanism without accidentally conceding that the DAO is already a legal association, partnership, joint venture, or other entity.

## 1. No admission by preparation

The framework should repeatedly distinguish between:

- a DAO's actual position that it is not necessarily a UNA or other legal entity; and
- a fallback position that applies only if a court, regulator, counterparty, or other competent authority characterizes the DAO as a UNA.

The documents should avoid language such as "the DAO is hereby formed as an UNA" unless the intended use case is affirmative UNA formation. EmergencyUNA is focused on defensive characterization and emergency continuity.

## 2. Emergency authority should be narrow

An emergency manager, committee, agent, or counsel should receive only the authority needed to respond to the defined emergency. The authority should be specific as to:

- triggering events;
- duration;
- reporting obligations;
- treasury limits;
- settlement authority;
- counsel-retention authority;
- communications authority; and
- termination or ratification procedures.

The framework should not silently transfer ordinary protocol governance to an emergency actor.

## 3. DAO roles are not interchangeable

DAO participants may occupy very different factual and legal positions. Templates should avoid treating all participants as equivalent. Relevant categories may include:

- passive token holders;
- token holders who vote;
- delegates;
- governance proposers;
- multisig signers;
- core contributors;
- foundation or company service providers;
- protocol developers;
- front-end operators;
- treasury managers;
- security council members; and
- informal community moderators.

The emergency framework should state which role is being addressed and why.

## 4. Jurisdiction must be explicit

UUNAA-style statutes are not uniform in every operational detail. A useful document should identify the jurisdiction being analyzed and should cite the relevant statute or primary authority. Statements about liability, property ownership, service of process, and litigation capacity should not be generalized across jurisdictions without qualification.

## 5. Preserve operational legitimacy

A court or regulator is more likely to respect an emergency process if it resembles the DAO's ordinary governance norms. Drafts should account for:

- governance forums;
- Snapshot or similar off-chain voting;
- on-chain proposals;
- multisig execution;
- emergency security councils;
- delegate voting;
- quorum and approval thresholds;
- public notice periods; and
- authenticated publication channels.

## 6. Separate legal defense from protocol control

The power to hire counsel, accept service, file pleadings, preserve records, respond to subpoenas, or defend litigation should not automatically include the power to upgrade contracts, drain treasury assets, change token economics, or bind the DAO to broad commercial obligations.

## 7. Counsel review is mandatory for real-world use

Every production use should be reviewed by qualified counsel. The framework implicates entity law, civil procedure, conflicts of law, securities and commodities regulation, fiduciary concepts, tax, sanctions, money transmission, consumer protection, and privilege issues depending on the facts.
