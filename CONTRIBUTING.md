# Contributing

EmergencyUNA is a legal-engineering research project. Contributions should be precise, jurisdiction-specific, and clear about what is settled law, what is commentary, and what is experimental drafting.

## Ground rules

Do not contribute:

- confidential client materials;
- privileged legal analysis;
- non-public DAO communications;
- settlement communications;
- sealed or restricted litigation materials;
- private keys, seed phrases, API keys, or credentials; or
- documents that imply real-world adoption without counsel review.

## Legal-source standards

Where possible, cite primary authority. Useful sources include:

- statutes;
- regulations;
- court opinions;
- administrative orders;
- official legislative materials;
- agency guidance; and
- filed pleadings or orders from public dockets.

Secondary sources may be useful, but they should be identified as commentary rather than authority.

## Drafting conventions

Templates should:

- include a prominent drafting note;
- use bracketed variables for facts that must be customized;
- identify the relevant jurisdiction;
- avoid implying that emergency procedures create an entity unless that is the intended use case;
- distinguish token holders, voters, delegates, multisig signers, developers, contributors, and service providers where relevant;
- include no-admission language when appropriate; and
- make clear that counsel review is required before use.

## Pull request checklist

Before opening a pull request, confirm:

- [ ] The contribution does not include confidential or privileged material.
- [ ] Jurisdiction-specific claims are identified and sourced.
- [ ] Experimental drafting is labeled as experimental.
- [ ] Any template includes bracketed variables where customization is required.
- [ ] The change does not present the repository as legal advice.
- [ ] The change preserves the distinction between emergency legal authority and ordinary DAO governance.

## Disclaimer

Nothing in this repository is legal advice. Nothing in a contribution creates an attorney-client relationship. Production use requires review by qualified counsel.
